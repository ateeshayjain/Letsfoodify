02 What the business actually is

D2C packaged food. Instant, preservative-free, home-style Indian food — mostly dehydrated product that reconstitutes with hot water. Three ranges:

  • Foodify Express — add hot water, ready in minutes. Positioned at travellers, students, the elderly, families.
  • Foodify Hot & Fresh — minimal stove cooking.
  • Foodify Flavors — chutneys, stir with cold water.

Observed SKUs: Dal Fry (Jain), Dal Makhni, Idli Sambhar, Pav Bhaji, Palak Gravy, Dal Khichdi, Aloo ka Mazaa, Super Millet Idli, Sprouted Millets & Beetroot Cheela, Masala Chai, Lemon Masala Tea, Coconut Red Chutney and chutney combos. Prices ₹100 – ₹1,800+, singles and combos, several on sale. Shelf life is 9–12 months and already published per product — which is exactly the field the e-commerce shelf-life rule turns on (§08).

Read on the buyer

A Jain variant, millet SKUs, and an elderly/traveller framing say the buyer is health-and-convenience led, skews 35+, and a meaningful slice is diaspora or travel-driven. That should steer both the design and the keyword set, and it makes the gifting/combo path worth merchandising properly. 03 Recommendations

R1 — Revamp on WooCommerce, don't rebuild headless

Every requirement in the brief has a mature WooCommerce path. The client's admin is non-technical and already knows this back office. A headless rebuild would roughly double build cost, add a permanent maintenance burden they can't service in-house, and risk the indexed catalogue for no commercial gain at this size. Revisit past roughly ₹50L/mo GMV or a multi-warehouse operation.

R2 — Treat it as a replatform of the presentation layer, URLs frozen

New theme, rebuilt templates, new design system; product and category URLs preserved byte-for-byte wherever possible. This single decision protects the "SEO" half of the brief — §07.

R3 — Coupon attribution: build thin now, buy the platform later

If coupon owners are a handful of known partners, a 4–6 day custom add-on does exactly what Nalin described with no licence fee. Move to AffiliateWP when they need self-service signup, payouts and 50+ partners. §06.

R4 — OTP login, with guest checkout kept as the default path

Mobile-OTP accounts are right for address reuse, but forcing registration is the classic Indian D2C conversion killer. Guest stays primary; the account is offered post-purchase — "save this address for next time".

R5 — "Google profile" is probably the wrong Google surface to lead with

Business Profile helps brand and local search, and their Noida address means they can hold one. But for a packaged-goods catalogue the surface that moves revenue is Google Merchant Center — free product listings and a Shopping feed. Do both, lead with Merchant Center. Say this plainly, because they asked for the other one.

R6 — Compliance display is a launch blocker, not a nice-to-have

FSSAI licence display, packaged-commodity declarations and the shelf-life rule are legal duties for an Indian online food seller. Build them into the PDP template rather than bolting them on. Client confirms specifics with their own FSSAI consultant — this scopes the build, it isn't legal advice.

Hard dependency, starts week 1

TRAI DLT registration for transactional SMS carries a 1–2 week lead time and is client-owned. It gates the OTP flow in W4. If it doesn't start in week 1 it becomes the critical path instead of design sign-off.

06 The coupon attribution engine

What was asked, verbatim

"Coupon code is essential so that an email is sent to the person who gets an email mentioning that a sales has happened using their coupon code (inventory & value). Also this should be visible on the backend dashboard."

Read: partner/influencer coupon attribution. Each partner gets a code. When a customer buys with it, the partner is emailed that a sale happened, with what sold and for how much. Admin sees the same, aggregated.

"Inventory & value" reads as which items and what quantity plus the order value. Confirm that reading — it could equally mean the partner's remaining redemption allowance. The spec below covers the first; Q10 settles it.

Custom if partners are few — under 20, known personally, not paid a per-sale commission through the system. The moment they want partners logging in to see their own numbers, or automated commission and payouts, buy AffiliateWP. Writing payouts, TDS handling and fraud controls from scratch is a bad trade.

Data model

Extend the native WooCommerce coupon. Don't invent a parallel discount system.

Coupon meta (on wp_posts / shop_coupon):
  _foodify_owner_name
  _foodify_owner_email        ← notification target
  _foodify_owner_phone        ← optional, for WhatsApp later
  _foodify_notify_enabled     ← bool
  _foodify_commission_pct     ← optional, reporting only in v1

Attribution record (custom table, one row per redeeming order):
  id, order_id, coupon_id, coupon_code, owner_email,
  order_total, discount_amount, commission_amount,
  line_items_json           ← SKU, name, qty, line total   ("inventory")
  order_status, notified_at, created_at

A custom table rather than order meta — it keeps reporting queries cheap and survives order deletion for accounting.

Trigger

Write the attribution row on payment complete, never order creation — an unpaid or failed order must not tell a partner they made a sale. Handle the reversal path: on refund or cancellation mark the row reversed and, per Q11, optionally send a correction. Silent reversal is how partner trust dies.

Partner email

Partner name, code used, order date, line items — SKU, product, qty, line value, order value, discount given, commission if configured, and month-to-date totals for that code. Plain, branded, mobile-legible.

No buyer PII, ever

No customer name, address, phone or email in the partner email. That's a privacy line and a DPDP-Act-shaped one. State it to the client explicitly so they don't ask for it in month three.

Admin reporting

A Coupon Performance screen — per code: redemptions, gross value, discount given, commission owed, last used, partner contact. Date filtering, CSV export, drill-through to orders. Summarised as a widget on the W6 dashboard.

Tests worth writing

  • Payment-complete fires exactly one notification per order
  • A failed-then-retried payment does not double-notify
  • Refund reverses the row
  • A coupon with notification disabled emails nobody
  • Two coupons on one order attribute to both without double-counting revenue
  • Deleting a coupon doesn't orphan history
  • The email contains zero customer PII

07 The SEO migration risk

Read this before quoting

A WordPress "revamp" is the most common way a store loses its rankings. The brief asks for a revamp and better SEO in one sentence, and those pull against each other unless the migration is a first-class deliverable. The catalogue at /product/<slug>/ is an accumulated asset. A new theme that changes permalink structure, drops the category taxonomy, or renames slugs "for tidiness" throws it away — and the loss surfaces 3–6 weeks after launch, long after everyone agreed the project went well.

Non-negotiables

  1. Freeze the URL structure. Product and category URLs preserved exactly. Any change justified individually and carrying a 301.
  2. Complete URL inventory before design starts. Crawl the live site, export every indexed URL with its GSC impressions. That list is the migration contract.
  3. A 301 map for every URL that does change, applied at cutover and crawler-tested on staging. Not "we'll add redirects after."
  4. Preserve title, meta and schema through the theme change. A new theme frequently drops the SEO plugin's output silently. Verify per template.
  5. Staging noindexed; verify production is not. A noindex shipped to production is the classic launch catastrophe. Explicit checklist item at cutover.
  6. Baseline before, measure after. W1 captures the numbers; hypercare compares against them. Without a baseline nobody can tell whether the revamp helped or hurt — which is how these projects avoid accountability.

Say this at kickoff. It reframes "why is SEO a separate line item" before it gets asked.

08 India compliance — food, sold online

Scope, not counsel

A build checklist. The client confirms specifics with their own FSSAI consultant.

  • FSSAI licence number displayed on the website and on invoices. Mandatory for a food business operator.
  • Packaged-commodity declarations per listing under Legal Metrology and the e-commerce rules: MRP inclusive of taxes, net quantity, best-before, veg/non-veg mark, ingredients, allergens, nutritional info, country of origin, consumer-care contact. These belong in the PDP template as structured fields, not free text — that way they're enforceable, and they feed the Merchant Center product feed at no extra cost.
  • Shelf-life-at-delivery. FSSAI requires meaningful remaining shelf life at delivery — commonly cited as 30% remaining or 45 days, whichever is less. Their 9–12 month lives make this comfortable, but stock rotation should surface in the admin so old batches aren't shipped. Verify the current threshold; the rule has been revised.
  • GST invoicing — compliant tax invoice per order, correct HSN and rate per food category.
  • DPDP Act — the OTP flow and coupon emails both touch personal data. Privacy policy reflects what's actually collected; partner emails carry no buyer PII.
